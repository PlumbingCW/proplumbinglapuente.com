Water Softener and Filtration in La Puente, CA

La Puente tap water is hard, and scale shows up on fixtures, in heaters and on glass. A licensed plumber explains softeners and filters and installs the system that fits your household.

Water softener and filtration service in La Puente begins with a question: what problem are you trying to solve? Scale on faucets and showerheads, spots on glass, a short water heater life and dry skin are signs of hard water. A chlorine taste or a musty smell is a different issue, which a carbon filter addresses.

A softener removes hardness minerals. A filter removes sediment, chlorine or other contaminants, depending on the type. They are not the same thing, and a plumber should recommend the one that fits your complaint instead of selling a package.

Hardness

How hard is La Puente water?

The La Puente Valley County Water District reported average treated-water hardness of 224 mg/L as calcium carbonate in its 2023 water quality report, with a range from 169 to 344 mg/L. That is hard water, roughly 13 grains per gallon on average. Not every address in the city is served by that district, so check your bill for your provider.

Ask your water utility for the report that covers your address, and test your tap with a simple kit if you want your own reading. A plumber can also test hardness on site before recommending anything.

Softeners

What a water softener does

A salt-based softener exchanges calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium as water passes through a resin tank. The resin is flushed on a schedule with brine, and the wastewater goes to a drain. The result is softer water that protects fixtures, heaters and appliances from scale.

Rules on softener discharge and salt use differ by place. Ask your water utility and the City of La Puente what applies to you. Salt-free conditioners are sold too, but they do not remove hardness in the same way, so ask what claims the maker can document.

Filters

Filtration: sediment, carbon and reverse osmosis

Whole-house sediment filters catch sand and rust. Carbon filters reduce chlorine taste and odor. Reverse osmosis under the sink treats drinking water in one tap and removes many dissolved solids. Each solves a different problem.

Filters need cartridge changes on schedule. Ask the plumber what the replacement cost and interval are before you buy, since upkeep is the real expense.

Benefits

What softening helps and what it does not

Softening reduces scale on fixtures and in water heaters, which can help extend heater life. See water heater replacement for how scale affects tanks. It does not make water a drinking-water treatment, and it does not remove chlorine.

If your water heater is making noise from scale or you are replacing one, ask about the anode rod and flushing. A softener is one part of the answer, not the whole.

Water in La Puente

The district that serves part of La Puente draws mostly on wells in the Main San Gabriel Basin, and its 2023 report listed a pH of 7.9. Water quality varies by provider, which is why your own address matters.

If your home is served by another provider, ask them for their latest water quality report. Do not rely on a figure for a different system.

Softener installs follow the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and drinking water treatment claims are tied to published standards. Ask any seller which standard a filter is tested to, and ask your water utility about softener discharge rules.
