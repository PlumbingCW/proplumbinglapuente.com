When to Use Plumbing Tape: A La Puente, CA Homeowner Guide

Plumbing tape is a small roll that solves one problem well. Used in the wrong place, it hides a leak or makes one. Here is where it belongs.

Written by La Puente Pro Plumbing · Updated November 2026

Plumbing tape, often called thread seal tape or PTFE tape, is a thin white film that goes on the threads of a pipe fitting. Threads on pipes are tapered, and they do not seal by themselves. The tape fills the tiny gaps and lubricates the joint so it tightens evenly.

It is cheap and easy to use, which is why it ends up in many DIY repairs. This guide covers where it helps, where it is the wrong choice and why gas connections call for different materials and a licensed professional.

Where it works

Threaded water fittings

Tape belongs on male tapered threads of water fittings, such as a shutoff valve connecting to a threaded pipe, a hose bib, a showerhead arm or a threaded adapter. It helps the joint seal and makes it easier to tighten and later take apart.

Wrap the tape clockwise as you look at the end of the thread, so tightening the fitting pulls the tape in instead of unwinding it. Use enough wraps to fill the threads without covering the first thread at the end, which can push tape into the pipe.

Where it does not

Joints that do not use it

Compression fittings seal with a ferrule or an olive, not threads. Tape on a compression fitting can prevent the ferrule from seating. Flare fittings seal metal to metal. Toilet supply connections and faucet supply nuts use rubber washers or cones, and tape does not belong there either.

Glued plastic joints are sealed by the glue. Soldered copper joints are sealed by solder. A leak at any of these is a sign the joint itself failed, and tape will only hide it for a while.

Common mistakes

Mistakes that cause leaks

Wrapping in the wrong direction, using too little or too much tape, and over-tightening a joint that is cracked are the usual errors. A fitting that keeps leaking after taping is often cracked or cross-threaded. More tape will not fix that.

If you have taped a joint twice and it still leaks, stop and look at the fitting. A cracked fitting needs replacing, and a corroded pipe thread may need a longer repair. Leak detection helps when the drip is not coming from where you think.

Gas safety

Gas lines are different

White PTFE tape is made for water. Gas connections need materials rated for gas, and gas work should be done by a qualified professional. A poor gas joint is a safety hazard, not just a mess.

If you smell gas, do not search for the leak yourself. Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Never use a lighter or a match to look for a gas leak.

Quick fixes

Tape is a fix for threads, not for pipes

A pinhole or a split in a pipe is a pipe problem, not a thread problem. Wrapping tape around a pinhole leak is a short-term bandage at best. If a pipe is leaking, close the valve and call a plumber. Burst pipe repair covers what to do in that case.

Step by step

How to wrap a threaded joint

Start with clean, dry threads. Wipe off old tape, dirt and pipe dope with a rag or a wire brush so the new wrap has something to grip. Hold the fitting with the first thread facing you and wrap the tape clockwise, overlapping each turn by about half its width. Pull the tape snug so it follows the thread valleys.

Most joints need only a few turns. Thread the fitting on by hand, then tighten with a wrench until firm, and stop. Cranking harder than needed cracks fittings, especially plastic ones. Turn the water on slowly and check for drips after a minute, then again an hour later. If the joint weeps, take it apart, remove the old tape and start over rather than adding layers.

Gas and permits in La Puente

If you smell gas anywhere in La Puente, leave the building and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside. Gas work on appliances and lines belongs to a qualified professional.

Plumbing and gas work can need permits. The City of La Puente Building and Safety Division handles building, electrical, mechanical and plumbing permits, so ask it what applies before you start a project that goes beyond a simple repair.

Gas piping is regulated separately from water piping, and the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), covers approved joint materials. Ask a qualified professional which material suits the joint you have.
