Kitchen Water Faucet Repair in La Puente, CA

Most kitchen faucet problems come from a few worn parts. Some are easy to fix at home, and some are a sign the faucet is done.

Written by La Puente Pro Plumbing · Updated November 2026

A kitchen faucet works hard. It runs many times a day, mixes hot and cold water, and sprays or aerates the flow. When a part wears out, you see a drip from the spout, water around the handle, a weak stream or a loose base. Knowing which symptom you have points you to the likely part.

This guide covers the common problems, what you can safely try yourself and when a plumber is the better call. It also explains how to decide between repair and replacement.

Drips

A drip from the spout

A steady drip after the handle is off usually means a worn cartridge, washer or seat. Faucet types differ. Compression faucets have washers, cartridge faucets have a replaceable cartridge, and ceramic disc faucets have a disc cartridge. Finding the brand and model helps you get the right part.

Close the shutoff valves under the sink first, then open the faucet to release pressure. Take the handle off, remove the retaining nut and pull out the cartridge or washer. Bring it to a store to match.

Leaks

Water around the handle or base

Water at the handle points to a worn O-ring or a cartridge problem. Water at the base can come from a loose mounting nut or a failed gasket. Water under the sink may come from supply lines, valves or the drain trap rather than the faucet.

Dry the area, run the water and watch where it first appears. If the cabinet floor is already swollen, close the shutoffs and call a plumber, because the leak may have been running a while.

Flow

Low flow and spray problems

Unscrew the aerator at the tip of the spout and rinse out grit and mineral deposits. Soaking it in vinegar loosens scale. If flow is still weak, check the supply lines for kinks and the shutoff valves to see if they are fully open. A pull-out sprayer hose can also clog.

If several fixtures have low flow, the cause may be the main supply or pressure. That is a bigger question, covered under water line repair.

Decision

Repair or replace

Repair is the right move for a good faucet with one worn part. Replace when the finish is peeling, parts are no longer made, the body is corroded or you have repaired it more than once. A new faucet also lets you pick a feature like a pull-down spray.

Installation takes a short visit. Our faucet repair and plumbing installation pages explain what a plumber does and how to prepare.

Shutoffs

Check the shutoff valves while you are there

The small valves under the sink are easy to forget. Old valves can seize or leak when turned. If a valve will not close, replace it before you start a faucet job, because you need to be able to stop the water. A plumber can swap them in the same visit.

Before you start

Tools and parts to gather first

Most faucet jobs need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver set, an Allen key set, a basin wrench for tight nuts, a small bucket and a rag. Put a towel in the sink so a dropped screw does not vanish down the drain. Take a photo of the faucet before you take it apart, because the photo is a map for reassembly.

Write down the brand and model, which is often printed on the body or on the box if you still have it. A plumbing supply counter can match parts from the old cartridge or washer. Buying a part without matching it is the most common reason a repair takes two trips.

La Puente kitchens and the age of the plumbing

La Puente incorporated in 1956, and a good share of its housing dates from the postwar decades. Kitchens in older homes often still have original shutoff valves and supply lines under the sink, which is a reason to check them whenever you work on a faucet.

Hard water is also part of the picture. The La Puente Valley County Water District reported average hardness of 224 mg/L in 2023, so aerators and cartridges collect scale. Check your bill for your provider, and clean the aerator every few months.

Faucets and shutoff valves follow the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A like-for-like swap is simple, and a layout change is a question for the City of La Puente Building and Safety Division.
