Hot Water Tank Heating Element Replacement in La Puente, CA

An electric water heater with a failed element gives lukewarm or no hot water. This guide explains the signs, the replacement steps and when to stop and call a plumber.

Written by La Puente Pro Plumbing · Updated November 2026

An electric tank water heater usually has two heating elements, one near the top and one near the bottom, each controlled by a thermostat. When one fails, you may still get some hot water, but not as much, and it runs out faster. When both fail, the water stays cold. Replacing an element is a well-defined repair, but it involves mains power and a full tank, so it deserves care.

This guide covers how to tell an element problem from other heater problems, what replacement involves, what a plumber checks while the tank is open and how local water conditions in La Puente affect element life.

Symptoms

Signs a heating element has failed

The classic sign is hot water that runs out quickly, because the lower element heats most of the tank and the upper element heats the top. If only the lower element fails, you get a small amount of hot water at the top. If only the upper element fails, the tank may heat slowly or not at all in the top section. Lukewarm water with no change after adjusting the thermostat also points to elements.

Tripped breakers, a thermostat set too low and a failed reset button can look similar. A plumber tests the elements with a meter, which is the only sure way to tell. Never test with the power on unless you know how to do it safely.

Safety

Safety steps before you touch an electric water heater

The heater is wired to a dedicated breaker, and the elements sit inside a tank full of water. Before any work, the power must be off at the breaker and confirmed dead with a tester. The tank must be drained below the element level before the old element is removed.

Skipping either step is dangerous. If you are not comfortable proving the power is off and draining the tank, call a licensed plumber. Our water heater repair page explains what the service involves.

Steps

How replacement works

The plumber turns off the power, closes the cold water inlet and opens a hot tap to relieve pressure. Next the tank is drained to below the element. The access panel and insulation are removed, the wires are disconnected and the old element is unscrewed with an element wrench.

A new element of the same wattage and type goes in with a new gasket, then the wires are reconnected and the panel closed. The tank is refilled with a hot tap open until water runs steadily, which purges air. Only then is power turned back on. Skipping the refill step burns out a new element in seconds.

While open

What a plumber checks while the tank is open

Sediment that settles at the bottom of the tank can bury the lower element and cause it to fail early. The plumber may flush the tank while it is drained. They also look at the thermostat, the temperature and pressure relief valve and the anode rod condition.

If the tank is old and leaking or corroded, replacing the element is a waste of money. In that case, water heater replacement is the better path. A good plumber will tell you which case you have.

Cost drivers

What affects the price

The element itself is an inexpensive part. Labor depends on tank size, access and whether flushing or other work is added. If a thermostat also needs replacing, the cost rises slightly. A repair on a heater near the end of its life may not be worth it, so ask the plumber for an honest comparison.

Hard water and elements in La Puente

The La Puente Valley County Water District reported average treated-water hardness of 224 mg/L as calcium carbonate in its 2023 water quality report, with a range from 169 to 344 mg/L. Hard water leaves scale on heating elements, and a coated element runs hotter and fails sooner.

Not every address in La Puente is served by that district, so check your water bill for your provider and ask for its latest report. If scale is a recurring issue, ask a plumber about flushing the tank on a schedule and about whether a softener makes sense for your home.

The element circuit is wired under the California Electrical Code, based on the National Electrical Code (NEC), and the tank follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). California Health and Safety Code section 19211 adds earthquake bracing for the tank.
