Electric Tankless Water Heaters for La Puente, CA Homes

An electric tankless heater heats water on demand with no tank, but it draws a lot of power. Here is what La Puente homeowners should weigh before choosing one.

Written by La Puente Pro Plumbing · Updated November 2026

An electric tankless water heater has no storage tank. When a hot tap opens, cold water flows through a heat exchanger with powerful electric elements, and the unit heats it as it passes. That means you do not pay to keep a tank hot all day, and you do not run out of hot water in the way a tank can.

The trade-off is power. A whole-house electric tankless unit can demand a large amount of electricity at once, and many panels need an upgrade to supply it. This guide explains the sizing, electrical, venting and maintenance points a La Puente homeowner should cover before buying.

How it works

What happens inside the unit

A flow sensor detects water moving and turns on the elements. A temperature sensor adjusts power to keep the outlet temperature steady. Because the elements heat water directly, there is no flame, no flue and no combustion air. That makes installation simpler in some homes.

The limit is how much water the unit can heat at once. Every unit has a rise rating, meaning how many degrees it can add at a given flow. Cold incoming water lowers what the unit can deliver, so sizing should be done for the coldest incoming water in your area.

Electrical

The electrical question comes first

Whole-house electric units need heavy dedicated circuits, often several. Your panel must have the capacity and spare breaker slots, and the wiring from the panel to the unit must be sized for the load. If the panel is small or full, an upgrade adds cost and time.

Ask a licensed electrician or your plumber to check the panel before you buy. Smaller point-of-use units serve one sink or shower and need much less power, which can be a good fit for a distant bathroom.

Compare

Electric tankless, gas tankless and a standard tank

A gas tankless unit can deliver more hot water at once, but it needs a gas line of the right size and venting. A standard tank is the lowest upfront cost and is easy to service, but it takes space and keeps water hot all day. Electric tankless sits in between, with no venting but a heavy electrical need.

Which one is best depends on your gas service, your panel, your household size and your budget. The page on tankless water heaters covers the gas and electric options and flushing, and water heater installation covers the standard tank path.

Sizing

Sizing by flow and temperature rise

Add up the flow rates of fixtures that run at once, such as a shower and a dishwasher, then compare that with the unit's capacity at the temperature rise you need. A unit that is too small gives lukewarm water when two fixtures run. Your plumber should size by your actual use, not by a rule of thumb.

Upkeep

Maintenance and scale

Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger and reduces performance. Many manufacturers recommend periodic flushing with a descaling solution, and you should follow the manual for your model. Inlet screens also need cleaning. Skipping upkeep is the most common reason tankless units underperform.

Local points for La Puente homeowners

Electrical work for a new heater circuit is the kind of project that may need a permit. The City of La Puente Building and Safety Division handles building, electrical, mechanical and plumbing permits, so ask it what applies to your project.

Water hardness also matters for tankless units. The La Puente Valley County Water District reported an average of 224 mg/L in 2023, so scale buildup is a real consideration, and flushing schedules matter. For gas questions, SoCalGas customer service can be reached at 1-800-427-2200.
