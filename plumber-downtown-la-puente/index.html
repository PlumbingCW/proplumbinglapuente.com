Downtown La Puente is the historic center of the city. The City of La Puente describes it as part of the original Town of La Puente subdivision, recorded in 1886, and it is the area around City Hall, the Library and the Senior Center. Main Street and First Street are the streets the city names as its main downtown corridors.

This page is for homeowners, landlords and small business owners in and around that district. It explains what the city has documented about the area, which plumbing questions come up in buildings like these, and how to prepare before you call.

The area

What the city says about Downtown La Puente

The city's Downtown Business District Specific Plan was adopted in 1994, and the city's 2026 public review draft of its Mixed-Use Places plan treats Downtown as a mixed-use area. That means shops and offices at street level, with homes allowed above them.

The same draft plan says the older neighborhoods around Downtown are mostly single-family homes, many dating to the 1950s or earlier, and that most of the city's housing came from the postwar building boom. La Puente itself was incorporated on August 1, 1956.

The city does not publish a street-by-street boundary for Downtown in the sources we reviewed, so we describe it by its anchor streets and buildings. If you are not sure whether your address counts as Downtown, call and give the street. The service is the same either way.

Older homes

Plumbing questions that come with older single-family homes

A house built in the 1950s or earlier can have had its plumbing changed several times. Some owners have replaced supply pipes, some have replaced the sewer line, and some have not touched either. The age of a house tells you very little about what is inside the walls.

The only reliable way to know is to look. A camera inspection shows the inside of a drain or sewer line, and a pressure and flow check shows how the supply side is behaving. A plumber can also tell you what to watch for without guessing at materials you cannot see.

Slow or repeating drains

If a drain clears and then slows again in a few weeks, the blockage may be farther down the line than the first clearing reached. Drain cleaning with a camera look can show whether it is a clog or a damaged pipe.

Wet spots and high bills

A damp patch, a warm floor or a water bill that jumps without a change in use can point to a hidden leak. Leak detection finds the source before walls or floors are opened.

Water heater age

Tank water heaters wear out. If yours is leaking at the base, making loud noises or running out of hot water early, water heater repair or replacement may be the next step.

Sewer line condition

If several fixtures back up together or you smell sewage outside, a sewer camera inspection shows what is happening in the main line.

Mixed-use buildings

Shops and apartments under one roof

Mixed-use buildings can put a restaurant or salon on the ground floor and homes above it. Drain lines in a building like that often serve more than one tenant, so a blockage on one floor can show up on another.

When a drain backs up in a shared building, tell the plumber which units are affected and where the water appears. That detail helps decide whether the problem is a fixture, a branch line or the building's main drain. Who pays for the repair depends on your lease or ownership terms, so check those before approving work.

Kitchens add grease to the drain system. A shop or restaurant owner who notices slow floor drains or a sewage smell should have the line checked early, because grease buildup usually gets worse, not better. Hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall when cable alone leaves residue.

Permits

Permits and who to ask in La Puente

Plumbing in California is built to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Whether a job needs a permit depends on the work, so ask the City of La Puente Building and Safety Division before replacing a water heater, a sewer line or supply piping.

City Hall is at 15900 E. Main Street in Downtown La Puente, and the city lists a main phone line of 626-855-1500. A plumber can tell you what is typical, but the city decides what your project needs.

What to do before we arrive

A few steps protect your property and make the visit faster. Do them if it is safe, and skip them if you are unsure.

  • Find your main water shutoff valve and turn it clockwise if water is flowing where it should not.
  • Stop using drains and toilets if sewage or slow water is backing up.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from wet floors.
  • Note which fixtures are affected and when the problem started.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside. Do not call a plumber first.
