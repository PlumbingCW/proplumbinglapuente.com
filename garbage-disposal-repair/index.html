Garbage disposal repair in La Puente handles the common failures: a unit that hums but will not turn, one that is dead, a leak under the sink and a drain that backs up into the other basin. Many repairs take less than an hour.

A plumber also advises whether to repair or replace. A disposal with a rusted housing or a leak from the body is not worth repairing, while a jam or a tripped reset is a simple fix. Installation of a new unit is included when replacement makes sense.

Disposal leaking or backing up: first steps

A disposal that leaks under the sink or pushes water and food back up into the kitchen needs attention the same day. Call the 24/7 line and a dispatcher arranges a licensed plumber. You hear the price before work starts, and these steps limit the damage while you wait.

What to do before we arrive

  1. Switch off the disposal at the wall switch and at the breaker before anything else.
  2. Close the shutoff valves under the sink, or the main water valve if those will not turn.
  3. Empty the cabinet, put a bucket under the leak and wipe up standing water so the cabinet floor does not swell.
  4. Do not run the dishwasher. Its drain empties through the disposal and will push water into the sink or onto the floor.
Hum and jam

Disposal hums but does not turn

A humming sound with no grinding usually means the flywheel is jammed. Turn off the power at the switch and the breaker, then use the hex socket on the bottom of the unit to rotate the flywheel back and forth. A wooden spoon handle from the top also works to free a jam.

Never put a hand into the disposal. After freeing the jam, press the red reset button on the bottom. If the unit still hums or trips again, the motor may be failing.

Dead unit

No sound at all

Check the wall switch, the outlet and the breaker. Press the reset button on the unit. If the power is fine and the unit stays silent, the motor or the wiring has failed. A plumber tests the connections and tells you if the unit is worth saving.

Hardwired units need an electrician in some cases. The plumber can say whether the problem is electrical before you spend money on a new unit.

Leaks

Leaks under the sink

A leak can come from the sink flange, the drain connection, the dishwasher hose or the body of the unit. Dry the area, run water and watch where the drip starts. A leak at a connection can often be tightened or resealed. A leak from the housing means the unit needs replacement.

Water under a sink can damage the cabinet floor quickly. If water is spreading, close the shutoff valves under the sink and call.

Smells and clogs

Odors and slow drains

Odors come from food residue stuck under the splash guard and in the grinding chamber. Cleaning the splash guard and running ice and citrus peel helps. A slow drain is usually a clog past the disposal. Tools such as a cable can clear it, and drain cleaning explains when a line needs more.

Avoid putting grease, fibrous scraps, coffee grounds in bulk and large bones in the unit. These cause clogs in the kitchen line.

Installation

Installing a new disposal

Installation includes removing the old unit, mounting the new one, connecting the drain and the dishwasher inlet if you have one, and wiring or plugging in the power. Horsepower, feed type and sound insulation vary. The plumber helps you select a unit for your household.

Ask about the dishwasher knockout plug. It must be removed when a dishwasher connects to the disposal, and leaving it in place causes the dishwasher to fail to drain.

Disposals and La Puente kitchens

Many La Puente homes date from the postwar decades, and kitchens in those houses often have older drain lines under the sink. Narrow or aging lines clog more easily when a disposal pushes food scraps into them.

Hard water also leaves scale on the dishwasher connection and the flange. The La Puente Valley County Water District reported an average of 224 mg/L hardness in 2023. Wipe the splash guard regularly and check the dishwasher drain for buildup.

A disposal drains under the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and its power connection falls under the California Electrical Code, based on the National Electrical Code (NEC). Ask the City of La Puente Building and Safety Division whether new wiring needs a permit.
