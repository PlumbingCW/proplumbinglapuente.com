Commercial plumbing in La Puente covers the repair, maintenance and installation of plumbing in restaurants, shops, offices, salons, medical suites and multi-unit buildings. The pipes are bigger, the loads are heavier and the cost of downtime is higher than at home.

Business owners want three things from a plumber: a fast response when something breaks, honest advice on repair versus replacement and minimal disruption to customers. This page explains what commercial service covers and how to prepare.

Business plumbing emergency: first steps

A backed-up floor drain in a kitchen, a failed restroom line or a burst supply pipe can close a business for the day. Call the 24/7 line and a dispatcher arranges a licensed plumber, with the price explained before work starts. While you wait, protect staff, customers and stock.

What to do before we arrive

  1. Stop using the affected fixtures and put up a sign on restroom doors if they are out of service.
  2. Close the main water valve if water is flowing from a pipe, and note where the valve is for the plumber.
  3. Keep people away from sewage and wet floors near electrical equipment.
  4. Photograph the damage and the time it started for your insurer and your landlord.

What commercial plumbing covers

Common calls include clogged floor drains and sinks, backed-up restroom lines, water heater failures, leaking supply lines, low pressure, fixture repair and replacement, backflow device checks and grease line problems. Planned work includes remodel rough-in, fixture installation and preventive maintenance.

Hours matter. Many businesses need work done before opening or after closing. Ask about after-hours scheduling when you call. For an urgent backup in the middle of service, the 24/7 line is open and a dispatcher arranges a licensed plumber.

Restaurants

Restaurants, grease and drains

Kitchens load drains with fats, oils and food waste, and floor sinks and lines clog on a regular cycle. Regular jetting keeps lines clear between problems. Hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall where a cable only opens a channel.

Grease interceptors and traps have their own rules. Ask the City of La Puente and the local sewer agency what is required for your kitchen. A plumber can help you understand the equipment, but the requirements come from the agency.

Multi-unit

Apartments and multi-unit properties

In buildings with several units, one blockage can affect many tenants. A camera inspection of the shared line shows whether the problem is a one-time clog or a structural issue. Sewer camera inspection explains what the plumber looks for.

Property managers benefit from scheduled maintenance, a record of what was found and a single point of contact. Ask the plumber to document each visit with photos and notes you can share with owners.

Water heaters

Commercial water heaters

Businesses with high hot water demand use larger tank units, tankless banks or boilers. A failed heater halts a salon or a kitchen. Planned replacement before failure avoids lost days. Water heater replacement covers the process, which is similar in scale though larger in equipment.

California requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion. Ask the plumber how this is handled for commercial units.

Businesses along Valley Boulevard and Main Street

La Puente's commercial strips along Valley Boulevard and in the center of town mix restaurants, shops, salons and service businesses, many in older buildings. Older buildings may have mixed pipe materials from different remodel eras, which complicates repairs.

Ask the plumber to note the pipe material and condition at each visit. Over time that record helps you plan upgrades instead of reacting to failures.

Commercial plumbing is built to the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with extra rules for kitchens and public restrooms that the city and the sewer agency enforce. Ask them what applies to your business.
