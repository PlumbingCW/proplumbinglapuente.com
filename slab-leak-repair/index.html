Emergency slab leak repair in La Puente deals with water escaping from a pipe that runs in or under the concrete foundation. Because the pipe is buried, the leak can run for weeks before it shows, and it can undermine soil, lift flooring and push a water bill sharply upward.

The first job is to confirm that the leak is under the slab and find its exact position. The second is to choose a repair that suits the pipe, the floor and the budget, whether that is breaking the slab at the leak, rerouting the line through the attic or walls, or lining the pipe.

Signs of a slab leak that need a call today

A warm patch on the floor, a sound of running water with everything off, wet carpet at the edge of a room or a meter that spins with all fixtures closed are urgent signs. Water under a slab can erode soil and damage flooring. The 24/7 line is open, and the dispatcher arranges a licensed plumber.

What to do before we arrive

  1. Close the main water valve to stop the loss, then check whether the meter keeps moving.
  2. Keep children and pets off any warm or wet floor area.
  3. Take photos of the damp or warm spots and note the time you first noticed them.
  4. Move rugs and furniture off the area and avoid running appliances that use water.
Detection

How a slab leak is found

The plumber begins by isolating the system to confirm the leak is in the pressurized lines. Then electronic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping from a pipe, and a locator marks the spot at the surface. Thermal imaging helps when a hot water line leaks, since the floor above is warmer.

This work limits the opening in the slab to a small area. The plumber should explain what they found and how sure they are before any cutting begins.

Options

Repair choices for a slab leak

There are three common paths. Direct repair breaks the slab at the leak, replaces the pipe section and patches the concrete. Rerouting abandons the leaking line and runs new pipe through walls or the attic. Lining or coating the pipe is possible in some cases but depends on the pipe condition.

Each has trade-offs. Direct repair is the fastest for a single leak but disrupts flooring. Rerouting costs more but avoids the slab and addresses a tired line. If several leaks have appeared, repiping is a bigger investment that solves the pattern.

Causes

What causes slab leaks

Copper lines in contact with concrete can wear from vibration and abrasion. Chemical reactions in the soil can corrode pipe from outside, and water chemistry can corrode it from inside. Settling and ground movement stress joints and bends.

Hot water lines tend to fail sooner than cold lines because of expansion and contraction. Drain lines under a slab can also leak, and they are checked with a camera, covered under sewer inspection.

Floors

Protecting floors and finishes

Tile, wood and carpet each need a different plan. Ask the plumber how much of the floor will be opened, whether matching tile is available and who handles the finish work. A clear scope before the job avoids surprises.

If water has already spread, flooring may need to dry or be replaced. A drying contractor can measure moisture and advise. The plumber fixes the pipe, but a drying company handles the structure.

Slabs in La Puente neighborhoods

Many La Puente homes were built on concrete slabs in the postwar decades, with supply lines laid in or under the slab. As those lines age, leaks become more likely. A house with original plumbing is a candidate for a slab leak sooner than a newer one.

If you are buying a home in the area, ask whether the seller has had slab leak repairs or repiping. A pressure test during the inspection period is inexpensive compared with a repair after closing.

Slab leak repairs follow the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Rerouting through walls or the attic is a larger scope than a spot repair, so ask the City of La Puente Building and Safety Division about permits, and call 811 before anyone digs outside.
