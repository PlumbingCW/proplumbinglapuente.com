Hacienda Boulevard is the main commercial corridor in La Puente. The city's draft Mixed-Use Places plan describes it as about 1.5 million square feet of floor space with more than 350 businesses, and credits it with over 85 percent of the city's sales tax revenue.

This page is for business owners, managers and property owners on that corridor. It is about commercial plumbing, not homes, and it covers the problems that shops, restaurants and offices run into and what to prepare before a plumber arrives.

Hacienda Boulevard also runs through Hacienda Heights. This page is about the part inside La Puente, and our Hacienda Heights page covers the neighboring community.

The corridor

What is on Hacienda Boulevard in La Puente

The city zones the corridor as General Commercial and calls it the Hacienda and Glendora corridor in its planning documents. Residential and mixed-use projects are allowed on some sites if they meet the city's criteria, so a property here can hold shops, offices or housing.

Public property listings show retail centers and commercial buildings with addresses on North Hacienda Boulevard in ZIP code 91744. We do not publish a list of businesses here, and we do not claim to serve any particular tenant.

Business needs

Plumbing needs of businesses on a commercial corridor

Different kinds of business put different loads on the plumbing. A restaurant sends grease and food waste down its drains, a salon sends hair and chemicals, and a retail shop mostly depends on restrooms and a water heater. The right plumbing fix depends on which one you are.

Restaurants and food service

Slow floor drains, sewage smells and grease buildup are the usual warning signs. Hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall, and a sewer line cleaning visit clears the main when a blockage sits downstream of the kitchen.

Retail shops and strip centers

Restrooms, mop sinks and a water heater serve most shops. A running toilet, a leaking supply line or no hot water can interrupt business even when the shop is otherwise fine.

Offices and salons

Shared restrooms and sinks mean one clog can affect several tenants. Salons add hair and product residue to sink drains, which can slow a line faster than a typical office sink.

Multi-tenant buildings

In a building with several businesses, a drain problem may start in one unit and show up in another. Tell the dispatcher which tenants are affected.

Tenants and owners

Tenant or landlord: who handles the plumbing?

In a leased space, responsibility for plumbing depends on your lease. Many leases make the landlord responsible for the building's main lines and the tenant responsible for fixtures inside the unit, but terms vary, so read yours before approving a repair.

If the problem is outside your suite, such as a main drain serving the whole center, contact the property manager as well. A plumber can document what is wrong and where, which makes that conversation easier.

Codes

Codes, permits and backflow questions

Commercial plumbing in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Ask the City of La Puente Building and Safety Division whether your project needs a permit, because requirements depend on the work.

Some businesses have backflow prevention devices that need periodic testing. Whether yours does, and how often, is set by your water provider, so ask them and check your water bill for the provider name.

What to do before a plumber arrives

If the problem is affecting customers or staff, these steps limit damage and speed up the visit.

  • Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and any sewage.
  • Close the shutoff valve for the fixture or the building main if water is flowing.
  • Stop using the affected sinks, floor drains and toilets.
  • Photograph the problem and note the time it started, in case your landlord or insurer asks.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside.
