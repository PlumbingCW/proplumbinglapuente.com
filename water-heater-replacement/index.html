Emergency water heater replacement in La Puente is for the tank that has failed, usually by leaking, and cannot be saved with a repair. The job is to drain the old unit, remove it, set a new one that is sized for your household, connect the water, gas or power, and test it before leaving.

Most of the decisions happen before any tools come out. The size, fuel type, venting and location all affect cost and performance, and getting them wrong means a heater that runs out of hot water or fails an inspection.

When a failed water heater cannot wait

A tank that is actively leaking is an emergency. Water can ruin drywall, flooring and stored items in a garage, closet or utility room within hours, and a pooled floor can reach electrical outlets.

The 24/7 line is answered at all hours, and a dispatcher can arrange replacement the same day when a unit is available. Here is what to do while the call is under way.

What to do before we arrive

  1. Close the cold water shutoff valve on the pipe feeding the heater. If it will not turn, close the main water shutoff for the house.
  2. Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or turn the gas control to off for a gas one. If you smell gas, leave and call 1-800-427-2200 or 911.
  3. Shut off power to any outlet or appliance standing in water, but only if you can reach the panel without stepping into the water.
  4. Move belongings out of the wet area and take photos of the damage before anything is cleaned up.
Sizing

Choosing the right size for your household

A water heater is sized by tank capacity and by how quickly it recovers. A common 40-gallon tank suits a small household, a 50-gallon tank suits a family of four, and a larger family or a home with a soaking tub may need more. A tank that is too small is the most common regret after a rushed replacement.

When the old tank is failing at 2 a.m., it is tempting to match whatever is available. Ask the plumber to show you the first-hour rating on the label of the new unit, because that number tells you how much hot water you get from a cold start.

Fuel

Gas, electric, tankless or heat pump

Swapping like for like is the fastest route, because the gas line, vent or electrical circuit is already there. Changing fuel type means new venting or wiring and takes longer. A tankless water heater saves floor space and heats on demand, but it needs adequate gas supply or electrical capacity and costs more upfront.

Heat pump water heaters pull heat from the surrounding air and use less electricity than a standard electric tank, though they need room and airflow around them and make some noise. Ask about them if you have a garage or large closet and a long-term plan for the house.

Code

Strapping, expansion tanks and permits

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, and says the minimum is to secure the unit in accordance with the California Plumbing Code. A replacement should come with proper strapping, not an afterthought.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of La Puente Building and Safety Division, which handles building, electrical, mechanical and plumbing permits, whether your swap needs one. A plumber who replaces heaters regularly can tell you what to expect and who to call.

Removal

What happens to the old tank

The old unit is drained, disconnected and carried out. The plumber should leave the space clean, with the floor mopped and the drain pan or area checked. Ask where the old unit goes. A scrap yard will take the steel, and a reputable plumber handles that as part of the job.

This is also the moment to replace the flex connectors, the shutoff valve and the relief valve discharge pipe if they are corroded. Re-using a rusted shutoff valve on a new tank is false economy, since that valve is what you will need to turn in the next emergency.

What affects the cost of water heater replacement

Replacement cost is not a single number. The unit itself, the labor and any code upgrades each move the total, and the plumber should give you one clear price for the whole job.

Unit type and sizeA 50-gallon tank costs more than a 40-gallon, and tankless or heat pump models cost more than either.
Fuel changesMoving from gas to electric or the reverse means new lines, vents or circuits.
Code itemsStrapping, expansion tanks, venting and discharge pipes add parts and time.
LocationA tight attic or second-floor closet takes longer than a ground-floor garage.
WarrantyLonger tank warranties generally come with higher-priced units.

Insurance note: a failed heater is wear and tear and usually not covered, but resulting water damage from a sudden rupture sometimes is. Keep photos, receipts and the failed unit's label until your claim is settled.

Replacing a heater in a La Puente home

Many La Puente homes have a water heater in the garage, which can hide a slow leak for a long time. Check the floor near the tank base when you park, because a tank that has been weeping for weeks is closer to failure than one that has just started.

Hard water shortens tank life. The La Puente Valley County Water District reported an average of 224 mg/L hardness in 2023, and heaters on hard water collect scale. A replacement is a good moment to ask about an anode rod check schedule and, if hardness bothers you, a softener. The water softener page explains the trade-offs.

A replacement heater is installed under the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and under California Health and Safety Code section 19211 for earthquake bracing. Electric units also fall under the California Electrical Code, based on the National Electrical Code (NEC).
