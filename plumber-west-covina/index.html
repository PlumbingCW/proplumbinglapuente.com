Problems first

The plumbing problems West Covina homeowners call about

Drain trouble leads the list. Kitchen lines carry grease, bathroom lines carry hair and paper, and the main line carries everything. When a main line is slow, several fixtures back up at once. The plumber cables or jets the line and, if the problem returns, runs a camera to see why.

Water heater failures are next. A tank that leaks at the base cannot be repaired and needs replacement. A heater that gives no hot water may have a failed element or thermostat, which is a smaller fix. Water heater repair explains how the choice is made.

Hills and pressure

Hillside streets, pressure and old supply lines

The San Jose Hills rise through parts of West Covina, and hillside homes can see a wider swing in water pressure than homes on flat ground. High pressure wears supply lines, hoses and fixtures early. Ask the plumber to read static pressure at a hose bib and say whether a regulator is installed.

Homes with original steel or early copper supply lines may develop pinholes. If leaks keep appearing in different spots, repiping may cost less over time than repeated repairs. For hidden leaks, leak detection finds them without opening walls.

Utilities

Which water provider serves your address

The City of West Covina lists several water providers in its utilities directory, including Suburban Water Systems, San Gabriel Valley Water Company and others, and the page does not say which serves each street. Check your water bill for the name of your provider, since it decides where the utility's pipes end and yours begin.

That matters for leaks near the meter. The meter and the line on the street side belong to the utility, and the pipe from the meter to the house is yours. Water line repair covers the owner's side.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A few quick steps limit damage and help the plumber start faster.

Stop the water

Close the main shutoff valve, usually near the street side of the house or in the garage.

Protect the room

Move rugs and furniture, and put towels or a bucket where water is falling.

Stay safe

Keep people away from sewage and wet areas near outlets. If you smell gas, leave and call SoCalGas or 911.

Take notes

Photograph the damage and note what you saw first. It helps the plumber and your insurer.
